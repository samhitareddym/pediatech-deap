# ReNu: 3-minute judge pitch (v4), speaker notes

About 20 seconds per slide (50 to 55 words), 10 slides, about 3 minutes. Press `n` in the deck to show these notes on screen.

## 1. Title

Hi, we're the ReNu team. ReNu is a medication app for teens with a kidney transplant, plus a portal for their care team. In one sentence: it keeps Cincinnati Children's MAPS loop (barrier, plan, follow-up) running between clinic visits, so the team doesn't have to staff every step by hand.

## 2. The problem in one number

Here's the problem in one number: about 40 percent of teens with a kidney transplant struggle to take their medicine. Their kidneys fail about three times as often as other age groups, and that usually means going back on dialysis. Most missed doses happen at home, between visits, where nobody on the team can see.

## 3. MAPS works, but runs on people

The good news: Cincinnati Children's already has something that works. MAPS cut late rejection by about half and saved about nine thousand dollars per patient. But every step runs on a person: a weekly meeting, someone typing in the checklist, a coordinator calling back. Even at CCHMC, 7 of 36 barriers never got a recorded next step.

## 4. ReNu keeps the loop running

ReNu keeps that same loop running without the hand work. A teen can tell us what's hard on any day, and the parent answers separately. Each barrier goes to its named owner from the 14-item checklist and gets one plan. The two-week check-back lands on the coordinator's list automatically. Simple barriers get self-help. Hard ones go to a person, fast.

## 5. Teen: Today check-in

This is Maya's Today screen. Twice a day she gets a check-in tied to her own routine, like after dinner. Every answer is okay, including missed or need help. Nobody's in trouble. And any day she can tap "tell my team what's hard." Those are the 14 checklist items in teen words, each one already routed to the right owner.

## 6. Teen: busy days (calendar + voice mock call)

Maya's hard days are the busy ones, and she doesn't want friends to see. So ReNu reads her calendar. When a band trip overlaps her evening dose, it warns her and lists what to pack that morning. She can also just ask out loud, and it sets up a discreet mock call from "Mom" so she can step away.

## 7. Care team: short trusted list

The coordinator gets one list a week. Six patients need her; everyone else shows why they're covered. Each card says why in plain words: ran out of tacrolimus, levels swinging, check-back overdue. Then one-click decisions: make a plan, log the check-back, refer, or snooze. The chart shows the whole MAPS loop per patient.

## 8. Your 5 questions

Your five questions, fast. Busy Tuesday: she opens This Week, six cards, one button each. "I refuse": it goes to a person within one to two days, never a chatbot. Parents see their own answers and what the teen shares, not the teen's notes. Month six: Grow chapters hand over responsibility step by step. No psychologist: one switch reroutes to social work.

## 9. How we would measure it

We'd measure the loop, not engagement. One: every barrier gets a plan. CCHMC's baseline was 29 of 36. Two: check-backs closed within 14 days. Three: coordinator minutes per patient. Our goal is to cut them in half. Four: steadier tacrolimus levels, the signal that actually predicts rejection. And the cost bar is generous, about 1,880 dollars per patient per year.

## 10. The ask

Our ask: let's pilot ReNu at one clinic, with one coordinator and 20 teens and their parents, for six months. We'd need your help setting the rules: who gets contacted after 48 hours, what counts as on time, and what goes in the chart. And like MAPS, we'd build it with a former patient on the team. Thank you!
